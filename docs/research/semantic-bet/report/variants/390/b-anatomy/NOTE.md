# B, "The anatomy"

The idea: each set is a band of its questions, one mark each, clustered and coloured by group; a retired question is hollow and struck, and a bar under a mark means its obvious SQL gives another number.
The hard set's two versions are drawn as one figure: v1's lane over v2's, with a thread from each v1 question to its copy (dotted where v2 relabels it), and v2's 15 new questions bracketed beside them; on a desk the threads stretch so each lane sits by its own facts.
Beside each band: what the set tests, written, scored and retired (and why, by class), the scored group mix, the frozen date and short hash (full on click), and whether the figures show it or the appendix holds it.
A reader scans the anatomy, hovers a mark to read its question, and opens it (click or Enter) for every turn, the numbers each turn must carry (rates as %), groups, kinds and SQL-rubric kinds in plain words, the obvious-SQL check, version lineage with a jump to its twin, the retirement reason, and every setup's runs in the unit plate's squares.
Chips (group, mark, kind) and a find box highlight marks across all three sets with a live count; arrows move within a band (up and down cross a thread), and an open card follows the arrows; Escape closes it.
A collapsed text list holds all 197 questions, filtered the same way.
Trade-offs: small marks (12 to 18 px pitch) favour the overview over touch comfort; the hard family's stretched threads cost height on a desk; at 390 px the facts and chips make the section long (about 2,800 px closed).
Groups take the scored labels where the register relabelled a question (the card says so); a v2 card shows its runs on v1 when the question is carried, since v2 is not in the figures.
The register's reasons are shown without tracker numbers or file names (a file name reads "the fixture").
Verified in Chromium (Playwright) at 1280 x 900 and 390 x 844, light and dark: no console errors, no horizontal scroll, text contrast at least 4.5:1 by a computed check, every interaction scripted.
Not verified: real touch devices, screen readers, and Safari or Firefox (color-mix needs a 2023 browser).
