## How the questions were written

- The first 59 cases are `questions.yaml`'s as of 2026-11-03, copied unchanged (the test asserts the copy).
- The 37 `bet_*` cases come from a subagent that saw only a business description written from Map #135 and the fixture's domains (entities, statuses, date ranges), never the metric list, `questions.yaml` or a schema (`measured`, this wake, 2026-11-03; the description is below).
  It wrote 30 questions, then 25 "ad hoc analyses a manager asks an analyst for", the second round asked for because the first gave only 10 a metric doesn't cover.
- Each `bet_*` case names its source in the set's `blind` map (`A<n>` first round, `B<n>` second); I wrote the expected SQL afterwards, seeing the schema.
- What changed between a blind question and its case, all recorded in `blind`: dates moved onto the fixture's days (returns, outreach, weather, two order questions where customers who signed up in 2026 first order in October), and one phrase ("placed at least one order" → "bought from us", which the existing set already uses for the same count).
- Dropped from the first round (30, 19 kept): 6 that repeat an existing case on other dates (fiscal Q3 revenue, the budget left on 30 September, the reply share, the objection share, the share of September's orders returned, total returns), 4 covered ones dropped so the uncovered share reaches 30% (revenue, average order value, site sessions, the add-to-cart-to-checkout share, all in September), and 1 on three people (applicants in September).
- Dropped from the second round (25, 18 kept): 1 that repeats `planted_top_customer`, 3 the fixture can't answer (returns per partner: returns carry no partner; customers with more than five sessions: none; candidates through more than two stages: the stage history is per snapshot), 2 whose coverage I couldn't settle (the booking rate among campaigns with 20 or more leads; replies per partner), and 1 covered one for the 30% share (the weekday with most calls).
- Coverage was judged after writing, by one rule: a case is `covered` when one metric query, with the semantic layer's dimensions and filters, returns the number; a share of a grouped total, a difference of two averages, a median, a percentile or a duration between events is `uncovered`.

## The power check (`measured`, [`probes/325_power.py`](../probes/325_power.py), 2026-11-03)

Simulated two arms on n cases with k runs each (a shared question difficulty, SD 2 on the logit; an arm-by-question term, SD 1; arm A about 73% right), and the rule's percentile bootstrap: the smallest true accuracy difference it calls a difference about four times in five.

| Cases | Runs per case | Smallest detectable difference (points) | Power there |
|---|---|---|---|
| 80 | 1 | 16.6 | 0.78 |
| 80 | 3 | 10.6 | 0.75 |
| 80 | 5 | 9.7 | 0.82 |
| **96 (the frozen set)** | **3** | **10.1** | **0.81** |
| 96 | 1 | 15.1 | 0.77 |
| 96 | 5 | 8.9 | 0.83 |
| 120 | 3 | 8.8 | 0.77 |
| 120 | 5 | 7.6 | 0.79 |

300 simulated evals per point and 600 bootstrap resamples each, bisected over the shift between arms, so each value is good to about a point.

- With the frozen 96 cases and three runs, a difference of about 10 points is the smallest the rule reliably sees; a smaller one mostly reads as "no difference", and the cost ladder then picks the cheaper arm.
- The spreads are assumptions: the more the arms agree question by question, the smaller the detectable difference; #333 can measure the real agreement from its runs and say how far off this was.
- The 2026-11-02 grilling's "10 points sits inside the noise of ~80 questions" holds for one run (16.6 points at n = 80, k = 1) and only just fails at three (10.6).

## What it means for the verdict

- The set is now harder for SQL than the old one in a way that matters: 11 cases where the obvious query is wrong by definition, and 29 where a semantic layer has nothing to say and SQL does, so the eval can show both what a metric layer buys and what it costs.
- A gap smaller than about 10 points between two arms is not something this eval can claim, so the verdict will rest on large gaps or on the confident-wrong rate.

## The description the subagent saw

<details>
<summary>The business, as given (both rounds; the second added the date ranges and asked for ad hoc analyses)</summary>

- A synthetic online retailer, in euros: customers in several European countries and Turkey sign up, then place orders with an amount, a status (placed, shipped, cancelled) and a date.
- Partners send back returns of orders, each with an amount, a reason and a return date.
- Marketing runs ad campaigns on channels with daily spend and a daily-snapshotted remaining budget; campaigns produce leads, some of which book.
- Customers act on the website and in the CRM (page views, add to cart, checkout, emails opened), grouped into site sessions.
- The partner team sends outreach messages to partner contacts, and partners reply.
- Candidates apply and move through stages (applied, screen, interview, offer, hired, rejected).
- Customer service calls are labelled with a topic and whether the customer raised an objection.
- Daily weather for Amsterdam and Istanbul; the fiscal year starts on 1 February.
- Rules: no files or tools, one number (or a named item and its number) per question, dated inside the data's dates, a mix of totals, shares, averages, medians, distinct counts, comparisons, patterns, ratios, durations and cross-domain questions.
