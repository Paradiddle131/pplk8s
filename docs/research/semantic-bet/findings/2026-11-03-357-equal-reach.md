# Equal reach for the data-agent arms, and what the generated context's verified queries carry (#357, Q8)

## Question

`native-context` (87.2% right at k = 3, [the #355 finding](2026-11-03-355-context-governed.md)) differs from `native-kc` (64.2%) in more than its context: it reaches 17 tables to `bet-kc`'s 7, and it carries 64 verified queries that may resemble the frozen questions.
How much of its lead is reach, how much the verified queries, and how much the rest of the generated context (glossary terms, the authored-context system instruction)?
And does the local baseline have the `legacy` schema in reach, as RULE.md wants for every arm that writes SQL?

### The baseline's reach

- `measured` (`tests/e2e/agent-fixture.sh` at 20:28Z and `tests/test_services.py::test_the_bets_baseline_reads_the_legacy_tables_the_fixture_built`): `dbt_dev` now holds `legacy.channel_daily`, `legacy.country_sales` and `legacy.daily_net_sales`, and `agent_sql` reads each (the grant is the fixture's, in `dbt_dev` only, so `dbt/macros/agent_warehouse.sql` and `platform/gcp/tests/test_access.py`'s "gold and exposed" stay as they are).
- `measured` (`services/semantic-agent`, `make agent-test`): the bet's runner (`semantic_agent.bet_eval`, `make agent-eval ARM=bet`) builds the baseline with the three legacy tables named after the catalog's in its instruction (`sql_baseline.agent.build_agent(extra_tables=...)`; the `execute_sql` tool now says "over the tables you may read" instead of "gold and exposed"), and logs each of its runs with `"legacy": true`; `custom-metricflow` keeps no legacy reach (its tools read only the semantic models, RULE.md § The arms).
- So the baseline runs logged before #357 (the 48 in `eval-local.jsonl` at this writing) had no `legacy` in reach and every later one does; the scorer tells them apart by that field, and eval-local.md and eval.md say how many of the baseline's runs had it ("N of `baseline`'s M logged runs had the `legacy` tables in reach"); the frozen set's 3 `distractor` cases are the only ones it can move.
- `measured` (the fixture run at 20:28Z): the Compose warehouse is shared, so from then on `dbt_dev` holds the legacy tables readable by `agent_sql` for every lane; a baseline run there from a branch without this change does not name them in its instruction but can find them through `information_schema`, and logs no `legacy` field.

## What it means for the verdict

- The managed data agent's lead over Knowledge Catalog's import is reach, not the generated definitions: on the same 20 tables Knowledge Catalog's arm is as accurate as our generated context (+0.003, no difference), and giving it those tables alone is worth +0.226.
  So Q2's and #354's "Knowledge Catalog leaves a gap" holds only against an agent given fewer tables; at equal reach the data agent finds most definitions on its own (the dbt descriptions on the tables, Knowledge Catalog's import, its own model).
- The verified queries carry nothing measurable on this set: dropping all 64 moved accuracy by +2.4 points (an interval from exactly zero to +0.052, no difference) and confident-wrong by none; in the 10 runs where the agent used one it was right 3 times.
  Whatever the frozen set's overlap with them (31 cases read alike, 64 have one computing their metric at their grain), it did not inflate `native-context`'s score.
- What the generated context does carry, at equal reach, is in the groups and not in the totals: every trap right and more follow-ups right, at the cost of `uncovered` answers; a verdict that weighs traps (definitions the schema cannot show) above coverage would still prefer it, but RULE.md decides on the totals, where the three arms tie.
- For the report (#336): the managed best is a configuration-only data agent with the warehouse's whole trusted layer in reach; a generator of data-agent context earns its keep, if at all, through glossary terms and the authored context, not through verified queries.
