# The blind judge on the turns the code cannot decide (#358)

Question: [`RULE.md`](../RULE.md) § Scoring a run sends a turn the code cannot decide to a blind judge, twice; until #358 every such turn counted as wrong.
Do the judge's verdicts move any arm's scores, and does any turn stay unclear?

## What was built

- [`bet_rule.py`](../../../../services/semantic-agent/src/semantic_agent/bet_rule.py): the judge's prompt (`judge_prompt`: the turn's question, the expected number in its first form, a rate also as its percentage, and the answer; never the arm, its model or its tool calls), the rubric as RULE.md words the four outcomes, `parse_verdict` (the reply's last `VERDICT:` line) and `two_verdicts` (equal verdicts stand; different ones, or a reply with no verdict, leave the turn unclear).
- [`bet_eval.py`](../../../../services/semantic-agent/src/semantic_agent/bet_eval.py): `judge_turns` asks every verdict the cache lacks for each logged `unclear` turn, with a `--requests` budget, and stops on a spent daily quota; `apply_verdicts` applies them on read, scoring each judged run again; `eval.md` and `eval-local.md` count the judged turns and list every turn still unclear (with its two verdicts, or "not judged yet").
  Run it with `python -m semantic_agent.bet_eval --judge --requests <n>` (the expected numbers come from `dbt_dev`, as for every other pass).
- The judge goes through the model factory: `model.build_judge()` is OpenRouter's free tier whatever `MODEL_BACKEND` the arms use, `JUDGE_MODEL_ID` overrides the default, and `build_model`'s `:free` guard refuses any other id.
  The default, `nvidia/nemotron-3-ultra-550b-a55b:free`, is a free model other than the one the local arms answer on (`nvidia/nemotron-3.5-super-vl:free`), so no arm's own model grades it.
- Each verdict is one line of [`judge.jsonl`](../judge.jsonl), keyed by arm, the arm's model, case, run, turn, a SHA-256 prefix of the answer's text, the judge model and the attempt (1 or 2), with the reply's tail.
  - Why beside the logs, not in them: other passes append runs to the arms' `eval-*.jsonl` while the judge runs, and writing a verdict into a log line means rewriting the file, which would race those appends (and conflict when lanes merge); a sidecar the scorer reads keeps every arm's log append-only and its own writer's.
  - The answer's hash in the key means a rescore or a later pass spends nothing on a turn already judged, and a changed answer (a rescored or re-asked run) is judged again; another judge model's verdicts are kept but not applied.
- Tests (`tests/test_bet_rule.py`, `tests/test_bet_eval.py`) with a stub judge: equal verdicts stand, different ones leave the turn unclear and listed, a second pass asks nothing, a changed answer is unjudged, a request budget stops and resumes, a spent quota stops with nothing written, and the factory gives a free OpenRouter judge even with `MODEL_BACKEND=gemini`.

## What moved in the scores

- Nothing that decides: `eval.md`'s accuracy and confident-wrong rate are unchanged for every arm (`measured`, the regenerated `eval.md` against the one at `7e2b835`).
  No judge verdict was right or confident-wrong, and an unclear turn already counted as wrong; declined and wrong count the same in both numbers.
- 12 turns moved from unclear to declined and 4 to wrong; no turn is still unclear, so `eval.md` lists none and says "Turns judged: 16; verdicts that stood: 16."
- `eval.md`'s old line naming three cases with an unclear turn (`bet_customers_september`, `bet_median_days_to_interview`, `bet_peak_page_view_hour`, run 1 only) is gone.

## What it means for the verdict

- The rule's default (unclear counts as wrong) did not bend any score here: the scores the rule decides on stand as the code gave them.
- `native-shaped` declines in words of its own 12 times: a reader of its group scores should know its "wrong" there was mostly honest refusal, which the rule scores the same as a decline on an `uncovered` case.

## Still unknown

- The judge's agreement with a human: no human graded these 16 turns; the two verdicts agreeing is consistency, not correctness.
- Turns logged after `7e2b835` (runs 2 and 3 of `native-context` and `native-governed`, the other lanes' passes) are not judged yet: `eval.md` lists them as "not judged yet" until `--judge` runs again on the merged logs.
- A hedge phrased outside the set's list (RULE.md's second judge case) is not sent to the judge: the code cannot tell it from a confident wrong number, so such a turn stays confident-wrong.
