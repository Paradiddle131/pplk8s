# The second, harder eval set, frozen before any arm runs on it (#356)

Question: were the arms asked the questions business users find hard, and if not, what set asks them, frozen before any arm sees it?
The owner, 2026-11-03: "were the agents asked such hard questions, difficult to measure and easy to fail due to the complexity of the business query, as well as the lack of knowledge around the terms? If not, I would really like to … add hard queries and measure accordingly" ([#356](../../../../../../issues/356)).
The frozen 96 cases mostly name one metric in its own words over explicit dates, with two ambiguous-term cases (`stated`, the issue's Evidence).

## How the questions were written

- Blind, from #325's business description only (the details block of [the #325 finding](2026-11-03-325-frozen-eval.md#the-description-the-subagent-saw)) and the issue's list of kinds: 52 draft questions in a business user's words, written at 2026-11-03T19:56Z before I read `dbt/`'s semantic models or metrics, the frozen set's questions, the eval's code or any schema (`measured`, this lane; the draft is below).
  Unlike #325, the drafting was not done by a separate agent: this lane had no subagent tool, so the same agent drafted, then read the fixture and the layer, then wrote the cases; the draft's timestamp and verbatim text are the evidence of the order.
  Before drafting I had read #325's finding, which names some frozen cases' topics (its trap table and dropped list), so a draft that repeats a frozen case's topic was moved or dropped afterwards (below).
- Then I read the fixture (`dbt/dev/fixture.sql`, `fixture_weeks.sql`, the built tables) and moved each kept draft onto days the fixture holds; each case's `blind` entry names its draft question (`J1` … `A6`) and what moved.
- Then I read the semantic layer (`dbt/models/semantic/semantic.yml`, the agent catalog's metrics, dimensions and glossary) only to classify coverage and name each covered case's metrics.
- Four cases were written after reading the layer, to keep the uncovered share well above 30% once drafts the fixture can't answer were dropped; `post` names them (`hard_roas_september`, `hard_social_vs_search_lead_gap`, `hard_leads_per_100_social`, `hard_weekend_vs_weekday`), so their coverage call is not blind.
- Coverage follows #325's rule unchanged: `covered` when one metric query, with the layer's dimensions and filters, returns the number (picking a row of one grouped query, as #325's rankings did, counts); a ratio or difference of two metrics, a share of a grouped total, a median, a percentile or a duration between events is `uncovered`.
  Two `uncovered` cases are one step of arithmetic from a single metric, so a semantic-layer arm can still answer them: `hard_cart_abandonment` (1 minus the cart-to-checkout rate) and `hard_leads_per_100_social` (100 over the cost per lead).
- Ambiguous turns keep the frozen set's shape (`ambiguous` lists the meanings' metrics, `expected_sql` gives one column per meaning, the answer asks which or gives every number); `hard_ambiguous_q3` (calendar Q3 or fiscal Q3) and `hard_last_seven_days` (today in or out) are two readings of a period, so their `ambiguous` lists one metric and their SQL two columns.

### Drafts dropped or merged (52 drafted: 39 used in 38 cases, a 39th blind case asks the drafts' own "Q3", 13 dropped; plus 4 `post`)

- Repeat a frozen case: J10 (median time to hire), J11 (partner response rate), S5 (the costliest return reason), the objection half of S6 (kept as positive calls).
- The fixture can't answer, or answers with a number an answer carries by chance: I5 (every customer of August and September ordered more than three times), X1 (all of them bought again within 30 days: 100%, which the eval accepts only as `1`), X4 and X5 (the same 10 customers; their real first order predates the data), I6 (two days of weather, both rainy in Amsterdam), R8 (no orders in the first fiscal half), M5 (hires per applicant repeats the still-in-the-running share), R4 (repeats J1's anchor and month), I7 (repeats I4's "real" reading).
- A3 ("conversion rate"): its readings are a lead booking and a site session checking out, and the second is no metric, so the turn could not list its meanings; left out rather than scored loosely.
- Merged: A6 with S2 (September's cancellation share is the same 8.33% in every country, so the conversation asks across 17 August to 30 September); A4 kept as the conversation, S1 as its own case.
- Kinds the fixture cannot hold at all: churn (every customer who bought in August bought in September), sell-through (no stock), CAC by acquired customer (leads and bookings are not customers), the same week last year (no 2025 data).

## What it means for the verdict

- The first set's 96 cases do not test what the owner asked: jargon, relative and fiscal periods, combined metrics, implicit definitions and ambiguity are nearly absent there, and here they are every case.
- 11 of the 12 `multi_metric` cases and all 6 `multi_step` ones are `uncovered`: a question combining two metrics is exactly what a metric layer's single query does not answer, so this set measures what an arm does past its metrics.
- Six traps bite on the fixture, one of them (`hard_turkish_vs_dutch_per_order`) picking the wrong country, not just the wrong number.

## Still unknown

- How any arm does on it: no arm has run on this set (no model was called in this lane), and `semantic_agent.bet_eval` reads only `semantic_bet.yaml` (`load_bet`'s default; no option names another set), so scoring the arms on it needs that option and its own results files.
- Whether the verdict rests on the first set, the second or both: RULE.md scores both under the same rule; which decides is the owner's call.
- Small-integer answers (`hard_customer_base_turkey` 5, `hard_active_customers_week` 10 and 9, `hard_runner_up_channel_leads` 20, `hard_top_channel_bookings` 31, `hard_mqls_social_september` 40) can be carried by chance, since the eval accepts any number in the answer; the drafts with the smallest such numbers were dropped.
- No distractor: `legacy.*` differs from the trusted layers only on cancelled orders and daily ratios, which the traps already test, and the legacy models were not built in dbt_dev this session.
