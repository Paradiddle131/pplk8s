Takeaways

  • The setup moves the score more than the AI model

    With one free model, the setups ranged from 61 to 95 right answers in 100. With one setup, 3 models ranged from 95 to 99.

    Same AI model, different setups0 ranged from 61 to 95 right in 100100
    Same setup, different AI models0 ranged from 95 to 99 right in 100100

  • A pricier model can score lower by following the rules

    Told to answer only through our metrics, every model got the questions a metric covers right (99 to 100 in 100). On the 13 questions no metric covers, the pricier models declined, as told, and so scored lower.

    Questions a metric coversQuestions no metric covers
    • A free modelQuestions a metric covers: 100 right in 100Questions no metric covers: 54 right in 100
    • Claude HaikuQuestions a metric covers: 99 right in 100Questions no metric covers: 54 right in 100
    • Claude SonnetQuestions a metric covers: 100 right in 100Questions no metric covers: 18 right in 100
    • Claude OpusQuestions a metric covers: 100 right in 100Questions no metric covers: 0 right in 100

  • Questions are hard to interpret only without our definitions

    Without definitions, Google's data agent fell from 80 to 64 right in 100 on hard-to-interpret questions, and wrong and sure rose from 19 to 30. With them it held at 100 and 99.

  • The program running the AI matters too

    Same tools, same rule, same model (Claude Haiku): on hard-to-interpret questions it got 84, 82 or 60 right in 100, depending on the program running it.

    • Google's agent kit (ADK)84 right in 100
    • Claude Code, run as a script82 right in 100
    • Claude Code, started by another agent60 right in 100

  • Each approach is strong on different kinds of question

    On everyday questions, the approaches ranged from 66 to 100 right answers in 100 where a metric covers the question, 60 to 100 where a hidden business rule applies, 38 to 100 where no metric covers it, and 29 to 100 on follow-ups.

    Each approach is strong on different kinds of question

    On hard-to-interpret questions, the approaches ranged from 48 to 98 right answers in 100 where a metric covers the question, 47 to 100 where a hidden business rule applies, 32 to 100 where no metric covers it, and 29 to 95 on follow-ups.
